# DS2 component A — CHUZC: the dual ratio test with bound flipping

**PROVENANCE: SOURCE-INFORMED (HiGHS). Not a clean-room result.**

Built by reading `/tmp/highs-study/HiGHS/highs/simplex/HEkkDualRow.cpp` under
the 2026-07-25 owner authorisation in `docs/PROVENANCE.md`. No HiGHS code was
copied; the algorithm was understood and reimplemented against linprogx's own
data layout and sign conventions. Nothing here may be reported as part of the
23 clean-room cells.

Date: 2026-07-26. Branch: `ds2/chuzc`.

---

## Verdict up front

**The no-sort structure works. The flips do not fire, and the per-call cost
win is not established.**

Three separable claims, in descending order of how well they are supported:

1. **A full bound-flipping ratio test costs +4% to +6% over an
   equivalently-scoped plain Harris test.** (Load-invariant cycle
   measurement, 470 real pivot rows.) This falsifies the campaign's working
   assumption that BFRT is inherently expensive. The existing `bfrt=1` path's
   cost was its per-pivot `qsort`, not the algorithm. HiGHS's sweep-based
   partition really is close to free.

2. **On the current formulation the mechanism is inert.** DS2 emits 0.064
   flips per pivot against the incumbent's 0.411. On 92–98% of pivots the
   breakpoint partition yields a *single group*, so there is nothing to step
   over. Cause, measured: only 2.4–17.8% of admitted candidates have a finite
   range, and an infinite-range candidate in the first group terminates the
   walk immediately. **I have no evidence DS2 would reduce greenbea's pivot
   count as it stands, and some reason to think it could raise it.**

3. **DS2 is *not* shown to be cheaper per call than the shipped kernel.** It
   is 0.77–0.97x of my scalar reimplementation of the incumbent, but the
   shipped incumbent runs an AVX2 kernel by default that my control does not
   reproduce, and that kernel is worth ~1.3–1.5x. Composing the two puts DS2
   at roughly 1.2–1.4x the shipped cost. See §5 — this is the weakest
   measurement in the report and I did not resolve it.

Recommendation to `ds2_core`: take this component, but do not expect a
trajectory change from it alone. Its value is contingent on a formulation
where nonbasic columns are boxed with small ranges — i.e. on the Phase-1
bound swap. Fund it there, or not at all. CHUZC is 9.7% of greenbea's pivot
(§5), so even a 30% saving inside it is 3% of the pivot; the target is pivots.

## 1. What was built

| file | what |
|---|---|

| `experiments/ds2_chuzc_validate.py` | harvest + replay + cost harness |
| `tests/test_ds2_chuzc.py` | 13 characterization tests pinning the mechanism |
| `src/linprogx/_csparse.c` | **observation-only** harvest hook, env-gated (§3) |

The signature is exactly as declared in the shared contract. The two inputs
the signature does not carry — the leaving row's primal infeasibility, and
the LU age that sets the minimum-pivot threshold — live in `ratio_state`,
which the contract declares component-A-owned. `ds2_core` writes
`state->delta` and `state->update_count` before each call.

### The algorithm

Four stages, no comparison sort anywhere:

- **Stage 0, admission.** One sweep of the pivot row's support. Keep column
  *j* when the *oriented* pivot `alpha_j * move_out * move_j` exceeds a
  threshold that **ramps with LU age** (1e-9 / 3e-8 / 1e-6 at <10 / <20 / ≥20
  updates) — a pivot that is fine on a fresh factorisation is not
  trustworthy after twenty. Track `work_theta`, the smallest relaxed
  breakpoint, where the relaxation is applied to the **numerator** as a
  dual-infeasibility allowance of `Td`. That is scale-correct; the
  incumbent's fixed 1e-7 relaxation of the *ratio* is not.
- **Stage 1, geometric pre-filter.** Partition against a threshold starting
  just above the smallest breakpoint and growing 10x per pass, accumulating
  absorption; stop as soon as the collected prefix can cover the whole
  infeasibility. Bounds the work before any breakpoint structure exists.
- **Stage 2, group partition.** Repeatedly partition the survivors against a
  threshold, setting the next threshold to the smallest *rejected* relaxed
  ratio. Each pass emits one group. Groups are ordered by ratio; members
  within a group are not — exactly as much order as stage 3 needs, at
  O(passes × candidates).
- **Stage 3, choose the group.** Scan groups from the **last backwards**,
  take the largest pivot in each, accept the first group whose best pivot
  exceeds `min(0.1 × max|alpha|, 1.0)`. Longest step first, with a *relative*
  stability guard. This is the load-bearing policy difference: the incumbent
  stops at the *first* slope-killing breakpoint and therefore always takes
  the shortest admissible step.
- **Stage 4, flips.** Everything in a group strictly before the chosen one
  flips to its opposite bound, for `ds2_core` to accumulate into one vector,
  one FTRAN, one combined primal update. A zero dual step (entering column
  already dual infeasible) discards the flip set.

### Deliberate departures from HiGHS

- **Candidates are a 32-byte struct-of-everything** (`alpha`, `tight`,
  `range`, `col`, `move`) built once at admission. The sweeps then touch one
  contiguous array; HiGHS re-reads `workMove[iCol] * workDual[iCol]` and
  `workRange[iCol]` through indirections on every pass.
- **Ranges are cached** in the component state (`ds2_chuzc_build_range`),
  rebuilt only when bounds change (a phase switch). Measured on degen2 this
  alone took DS2 from 1.26x to 1.08x of a pattern-scoped Harris test — the
  two random loads from `lo_ext`/`hi_ext` per candidate were the single
  largest cost in stage 0.
- **Ties on |alpha| resolve to the lowest column index.** HiGHS uses a stored
  random permutation. Determinism is a linprogx contract.
- **Columns carrying big-M artificial bounds are given an infinite range**
  rather than being excluded from the candidate list. Infinite range
  terminates the walk at that column, which is the correct semantics: you may
  pivot on it, you may not step over it. (The incumbent's `flippable`
  predicate excludes them from the candidate set instead.)
- **Both sweep loops carry a hard pass cap.** HiGHS's stage-1 loop has no
  termination proof when the initial threshold is non-positive, which a
  dual-infeasible column can cause; the cap makes the routine total.

`theta_dual` is returned in **linprogx's** convention,
`-r_q / (sigma * alpha_q)`, not HiGHS's `r_q / alpha_q`, so `ds2_core` can use
it directly.

## 2. The validation harness

`experiments/ds2_chuzc_validate.py`:

1. Runs the **shipped** dual simplex on real LPnetlib instances with
   `LINPROGX_DS2_DUMP` set, harvesting, for a strided sample of pivots, the
   complete ratio-test input state (pivot row support and values, all reduced
   costs, bound statuses, bounds, artificial-bound mask, leaving sigma, the
   row's primal infeasibility, the EXPAND tolerance, the LU age) plus the
   decision the shipped test made (entering column, pivot, dual step, flip
   set).
2. Replays each harvested pivot through four arms:

| arm | what |
|---|---|
| `harris_dense` | reimplementation of the incumbent, scanning all `n_total` columns as it does. **Agreement with the recorded decision is the fidelity check.** |
| `harris_pattern` | the same test restricted to the pivot row's support. Isolates scan shape from algorithm. |
| `ds2` | the component under test |
| `ds2_noart` | **counterfactual, not a legal configuration**: DS2 with the big-M boxes treated as flippable. Isolates "the mechanism does not fire" from "the formulation gives it nothing to fire on". |

3. Times each arm with `rdtsc` (minimum of `--repeat` runs), which is
   load-invariant, per the measurement doctrine.

Command as run:

```
uv run python experiments/ds2_chuzc_validate.py \
    --instances greenbea degen2 25fv47 --stride 17 --max-cases 200 --repeat 15
```

Raw output is committed as `experiments/ds2_chuzc_2026_07_26.json`.

## 3. The harvest hook does not perturb the solve

The harvest needed real pivot rows, which meant a hook inside
`CSRMatrix_solve_eq_box_dual_simplex`. Everything it does is behind a pointer
that is `NULL` unless `LINPROGX_DS2_DUMP` is set, and it reads solver state
without writing any. Proven with the trace-hash oracle, which folds the raw
bits of every BTRAN/FTRAN output into an FNV-1a digest:

| instance | digest before | digest after | digest with the dump **active** | iterations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| greenbea | `f3b45e49b8b83dd7` | `f3b45e49b8b83dd7` | `f3b45e49b8b83dd7` | 6,424 → 6,424 |
| 25fv47 | `7a4a1a166166c17d` | `7a4a1a166166c17d` | — | 8,350 → 8,350 |
| degen2 | `68405a1ad191be04` | `68405a1ad191be04` | — | 1,825 → 1,825 |

(Direct `solve_eq_box_dual_simplex` calls, `leaving_rule=1, expand=1`, no
presolve; these are not the `docs/DS2-REWRITE.md` route figures, they are a
fixed reference configuration for the invariance check.)

Bit-identical, including while the dump is writing. The shipped dual simplex
is untouched.

## 4. Validation result

Harvested from the public `algorithm="dual_simplex"` route (greenbea 6,807
pivots, 25fv47 5,144, degen2 1,174 — all `optimal`).

### Fidelity of the control

| instance | `harris_dense` reproduces the shipped entering column |
|---|---|
| greenbea | 200 / 200 |
| degen2 | 70 / 70 |
| 25fv47 | 200 / 200 |

**470 / 470.** The control is the incumbent, so comparisons against it mean
something. `harris_pattern` also reproduces it 470/470, which independently
says the shipped test's dense `0..n_total` scan finds nothing the pivot row's
support does not.

### DS2 versus the shipped decision

| instance | same entering column | differences | of which DS2 took a strictly longer dual step |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| greenbea | 186 / 200 | 14 | 14 |
| degen2 | 60 / 70 | 10 | 10 |
| 25fv47 | 197 / 200 | 3 | 3 |

**443 / 470 (94.3%) identical; every one of the 27 differences is DS2
choosing a longer step, and every one fired at least one flip.** That is the
mechanism doing exactly and only what it is for. There is no unexplained
divergence.

On the calls where both picked the same column and DS2 flipped nothing,
`theta_dual` agrees to **0.000e+00 relative error** — bit-identical.

One documented behavioural difference: on 33/200 greenbea calls (12/200 on
25fv47, 0/70 on degen2) DS2 clamps the dual step to zero where the incumbent
computes a negative one. These are calls where the entering column is already
dual infeasible — `r_q` on the wrong side of its bound, which EXPAND
deliberately admits. A negative dual step is not meaningful; HiGHS clamps
here too, and the incumbent then floors the step back up with its EXPAND
minimum. DS2's behaviour is the more defensible of the two, and `ds2_core`
must apply its own step floor on top.

### Soundness

Seven invariants checked on all 470 cases:

- the entering column is admissible under the sign rule
- its |alpha| exceeds the age-ramped threshold
- the dual step is never negative
- no flipped column is the entering column
- every flipped column is genuinely boxed
- no flipped column is in the never-flip mask
- **every flipped column's ratio is at or below the entering column's** —
  i.e. the flips are breakpoints the dual step actually passes, which is the
  substantive correctness property of a bound-flipping test

**Zero violations.**

Plus 13 hand-built characterization tests in `tests/test_ds2_chuzc.py`,
covering the longest-step walk, one-sided termination, the never-flip mask,
the age ramp, the zero-step discard, the large-alpha backoff, and
cache/no-cache equivalence. All pass.

## 5. Cost

rdtsc, minimum of 15 runs per call, median over cases. TSC calibrated at
**3.5932 GHz** (invariant TSC; the same value on repeat).

| instance | cases | n_total | mean row nnz | `harris_dense` | `harris_pattern` | `ds2` | ds2 / dense | ds2 / pattern |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| greenbea | 200 | 5,393 | 2,984 | 28,890 | 20,844 | 22,158 | **0.767** | **1.063** |
| degen2 | 70 | 1,143 | 792 | 5,886 | 5,454 | 5,688 | **0.966** | **1.043** |
| 25fv47 | 200 | 2,508 | 1,856 | 16,056 | 14,670 | 15,444 | **0.962** | **1.053** |

In wall terms greenbea's CHUZC is 8.04 µs scalar-incumbent versus 6.17 µs
DS2.

Two readings, and they say different things:

- **Algorithm for algorithm (`ds2 / harris_pattern`): +4.3% to +6.3%.** A
  complete bound-flipping test — admission, geometric pre-filter, group
  partition, backward large-alpha scan, flip set — for a 5% surcharge over a
  plain min-ratio Harris test on the same candidate set. **This is the
  positive result of the whole exercise.** The existing `bfrt=1` path was
  measured at 411 → 531 ms on greenbea for a −101-pivot gain; its cost was
  the `qsort`. Remove the sort and the algorithm is nearly free.
- **Against the incumbent as I reimplemented it (`ds2 / harris_dense`):
  0.77–0.97x.** But almost all of that is the scan shape, not the algorithm:
  `harris_pattern` alone is 0.72–0.93x. Scanning the pivot row's support
  instead of all `n_total` columns is a change available to the incumbent
  too, and it is where the saving lives.

### The caveat that undermines the second reading

The shipped ratio test does **not** run the scalar code my control
reproduces. When `bfrt == 0` and AVX2 is present — the default — it dispatches
to `ds_harris_pass1_avx2` / `ds_harris_pass2_avx2`. My controls are scalar.

Interleaved A/B of the shipped solver with `LINPROGX_DS_SIMD=1` vs `=0`,
five paired runs, `ratio_test` phase, µs/pivot (minimum, which is the
load-robust statistic here):

| instance | SIMD | scalar | scalar / SIMD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| greenbea | 27.49 | 41.99 | 1.53 |
| 25fv47 | 20.04 | 29.92 | 1.49 |
| degen2 | 7.15 | 9.31 | 1.30 |

Composing: DS2 lands at roughly **1.18x (greenbea), 1.26x (degen2), 1.43x
(25fv47)** of the shipped kernel. I am reporting this as an estimate, not a
measurement: it multiplies a clean cycle-level ratio by a wall-level ratio
taken on a loaded box, and the `ratio_test` phase bucket contains more than
the scan (flip execution, the FTRAN for flips, the `alpha_scratch` cleanup),
so the two are not measuring the same span. The arithmetic does not fully
reconcile — 8.04 µs of scalar scan cannot by itself account for a 14.5 µs
SIMD saving — and I did not resolve where the discrepancy lives.

**The defensible statement is the narrow one: DS2 is not established as
cheaper per call than the shipped kernel. What is established is that the
BFRT algorithm costs ~5% over a plain Harris test at equal vectorisation.**
DS2's stage-0 admission sweep is the bulk of its work and is structurally
vectorisable in the same way; that is unbuilt and unmeasured.

### How much is at stake anyway

greenbea phase profile, same run (loaded box, so read the *shares*, not the
absolute µs):

| phase | µs/pivot | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| btran_rho | 65.18 | 21.4% |
| pivot_row | 63.16 | 20.8% |
| ftran_col | 50.33 | 16.6% |
| rcost_update | 37.02 | 12.2% |
| refactor | 33.63 | 11.1% |
| **ratio_test** | **29.48** | **9.7%** |
| everything else | 25.16 | 8.2% |

CHUZC is under a tenth of the pivot. A 30% saving inside it is 3% of the
pivot. **Per-call CHUZC cost is not where a 1.5x trajectory deficit is
recovered.** The reason to want this component is the pivot count.

## 6. Why the flips do not fire — measured, not assumed

This is the finding that matters most for `ds2_core`.

| instance | DS2 flips / sampled pivot | incumbent flips / sampled pivot | counterfactual (`ds2_noart`) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| greenbea | 15 / 200 = 0.075 | 105 / 200 = 0.525 | 204 / 200 = 1.02 |
| degen2 | 12 / 70 = 0.171 | 24 / 70 = 0.343 | 140 / 70 = 2.00 |
| 25fv47 | 3 / 200 = 0.015 | 64 / 200 = 0.320 | 151 / 200 = 0.76 |

**DS2 flips 5–20x less than the incumbent, and 13–50x less than it would if
the big-M boxes were genuine bounds.**

The structural census says why:

| instance | admitted candidates | of which finite range | pivots yielding a **single** breakpoint group |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| greenbea | 544 | 27.5 (4.6%) | 186 / 200 |
| degen2 | 140 | 24.7 (17.8%) | 60 / 70 |
| 25fv47 | 435 | 10.2 (2.4%) | 196 / 200 |

And the absorption available to flips, against the infeasibility they would
have to cover: the finite-range candidates' *total* absorption reaches the
row's infeasibility on **0/200** greenbea calls, 17/70 degen2, 14/200 25fv47.
On greenbea the median ratio of available absorption to required is
**0.0** — for more than half of greenbea's pivots there is literally nothing
flippable in the row at all.

The mechanism: 95%+ of admitted candidates are one-sided or big-M-boxed, so
they carry an infinite range. An infinite-range candidate in the first
breakpoint group drives the accumulated absorption to infinity, the partition
terminates after one group, and a single group means nothing lies strictly
before the chosen one — no flips, and the longest step collapses to the
shortest. On **92–98%** of pivots that is what happens.

This is the S3 study's finding #2, now quantified from the inside: *"linprogx
installs big-M boxes on exactly the one-sided columns whose reduced cost
points at infinity, and HiGHS's Phase 1 makes every column boxed with range
≤ 2, so linprogx's flip mechanism is structurally inert precisely where
HiGHS's is maximally active."* The counterfactual arm is the direct
measurement of that sentence: make the ranges finite and the same code fires
13x more flips.

### The incumbent flips more, and that is not a contradiction

The shipped test's sweep 1 flips boxed candidates sitting **at** the minimum
ratio whose absorption is below the remaining infeasibility. DS2 flips only
breakpoints the dual step strictly **passes**. Those are different rules: the
incumbent's flips are legitimate but do not lengthen the step, so they cannot
buy the pivot reduction a longest-step test is supposed to buy. I am not
adjudicating which is better — that would need an end-to-end pivot count,
which needs `ds2_core` — but it does mean **DS2 as it stands would probably
not reduce pivots on the current formulation, and might increase them**: on
5–7% of pivots it takes a longer step at a sometimes materially smaller pivot
(greenbea iter 1326: |alpha| 1.29 where the incumbent took 4.71, both inside
the 10%-of-max guard).

## 7. Honest limits

- **No end-to-end pivot count.** This component cannot be run in a solve loop
  until `ds2_core` exists. Everything here is per-call. The claim the
  component actually needs to justify — fewer pivots — is untested.
- **The cost comparison is against my reimplementation of the incumbent, not
  the incumbent.** Fidelity is established at the level of *decisions*
  (470/470), not of *cycles*. The shipped kernel's AVX2 path is not
  reproduced, and §5 does not fully reconcile.
- **Sampling.** 200 pivots strided across each solve, not all of them. The
  degen2 numbers moved noticeably between a stride-5 (70 cases) and a
  stride-17 (70 cases) sample — `ds2 / harris_dense` 0.775 vs 0.966 — so
  treat single-instance cost ratios as ±20%, and the cross-instance pattern
  as the signal.
- **The harvest runs the public `dual_simplex` route**, not the
  `leaving_rule=1, expand=1` auto-route configuration behind the 4,399-pivot
  greenbea figure of record. The ratio-test inputs are drawn from a different
  trajectory than the one DS2 will ultimately have to beat.
- **`ds2_noart` is a counterfactual, not a result.** It is not a legal solver
  configuration — flipping onto a big-M artificial bound is meaningless. It
  exists only to attribute the inertness.

## 8. What would change the verdict

In order of expected value:

1. **The Phase-1 bound swap** (`ds2_core` / the S1 study's Step B). Every
   column boxed with range ≤ 2 is the condition under which this component
   becomes productive, and the counterfactual arm quantifies how much: 13–50x
   more flips. Without it this component is a ~5%-of-a-tenth-of-a-pivot
   change. Fund it there.
2. **Vectorise stage 0.** It is a single sweep over the pivot row's support
   with an eight-way branch, the same shape the incumbent already vectorises.
   This is the only credible route to making DS2 cheaper per call than the
   shipped AVX2 kernel, and it would close §5's open question.
3. **Reconcile §5 properly** — an rdtsc bracket around the shipped ratio test
   itself, both SIMD arms, in one process. Two hours of work and it turns the
   weakest number in this report into a real one.

Not worth doing: tuning the 10%-of-max stability guard or the group-partition
thresholds. Those are global constants in a mechanism that fires on 6% of
pivots; there is nothing there to find until (1) lands.
